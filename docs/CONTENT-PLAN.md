# Content plan — melanotanii.com

Working brief for expanding Tan Lines / The Record from its launch set (21 articles,
~400–600 words each, 5 clusters) toward deeper, more durable coverage.

**How to use this:** each proposed article below is a commissioning brief,
not a draft. Hand a brief to a council correspondent, get the substantive
copy back, then it gets edited into house voice and shipped through the
normal frontmatter/PR flow (see README "Adding an article").

## Non-negotiables (carry over from README)

- No sourcing, dosing, or usage guidance for unapproved compounds — ever.
- No affiliate links, vendor referrals, discount codes, or "researcher
  verification" gates on this domain. A reader looking for a source is
  not a reader this site serves. Commerce stays off, including when it
  is framed as help.
- Harms reported plainly; uncertainty stated, not smoothed over.
- The future-agonist thesis is opinion and is labeled as such in-article.
- Every article should survive a regulator, a dermatologist, and a
  skeptical journalist reading it.
- **Do not** stamp `updatedDate` unless the body was genuinely revised with
  new substance. Freshness signals must be real.

### `history` — "A drug before its receptor: how the Arizona team built Melanotan in the dark"
**Shipped 25 Sep 2026** as `/articles/before-the-receptor/` (PR #27) — this
filled the sixth `history` slot and closed the launch content plan. The
brief below is preserved as the commissioning record; do not restamp
`updatedDate` unless new primary literature on the discovery chemistry
itself lands.

Fills the sixth `history` slot with the discovery chemistry itself — the one
part of the origin story no live article owns. `where-melanotan-came-from`
names Hruby and Hadley and the analog program in passing; `who-owned-melanotan`
follows the patents; `one-hormone-five-receptors` (science) explains the
receptors. None of them tells *how* a peptide becomes a drug when the target
hasn't been discovered yet.

The brief: natural α-MSH is a 13-amino-acid linear peptide that is useless as a
medicine — serum proteases degrade it in minutes. The Arizona team's problem was
pure medicinal chemistry: stabilize it, potentiate it, keep it from wandering.
Their instrument was a bioassay, not a receptor — the frog-skin (and lizard-skin)
melanophore assay, in which α-MSH disperses pigment granules and darkens the skin.
From that bench came NDP-MSH ([Nle⁴, D-Phe⁷]α-MSH — Sawyer et al., *PNAS* 1980):
two substitutions (norleucine for methionine at 4, D-phenylalanine for
L-phenylalanine at 7) that made a "superpotent, ultralong" melanotropin, 26×
α-MSH in the adenylate-cyclase assay and active at 1/1000 the dose. The
His–Phe–Arg–Trp message sequence was the pharmacophore being amplified; cyclization
and further design produced the cyclic analogs that became Melanotan II. Only in
1992 — Mountjoy et al., *Science* — were the melanocortin receptors (MC1R/MC2R)
cloned, revealing, a dozen years late, what the bioassay had been reading all along.

Constraints: history, not a tutorial — keep receptor science to what the chemists
knew at the time (almost nothing). Do not re-explain MC1R signaling (owned by
`one-hormone-five-receptors`). The erection accident and the gray market are
downstream chapters other articles already own; reference, don't retell. Do not
restamp `updatedDate` unless new primary literature on the discovery chemistry
itself lands.

## Next wave — the α-MSH understanding cluster (briefs, 13 Nov 2026)

Audit finding (13 Nov 2026): α-MSH is named in ~15 live articles but is at
home in none. It appears only as the foil for Melanotan II — the natural
signal the analog was built to out-do — and no page explains the hormone on
its own terms. The science-cluster blurb promises "Melanocortin receptors,
α-MSH, and how the tanning signal really works," yet the gateway
(`one-hormone-five-receptors`) names α-MSH once. The analog is better
explained than the original: `before-the-receptor` covers what the chemists
did *to* α-MSH, `a-drug-with-no-address` covers how the copy misbehaves, but
nothing covers the molecule they started from.

The through-line for all three briefs is **potency, and why potency is the
whole story of tanning pharmacology**. Natural α-MSH is a weak, short-lived,
locally-released signal — the body's design, not a flaw. The Arizona program's
achievement was *super-potency*: NDP-MSH ran ~26× α-MSH in the
adenylate-cyclase assay and was active at ~1/1000 the dose
(Sawyer et al., *PNAS* 1980), and the cyclic analogs that became Melanotan II
carried that potency into a molecule that resists breakdown. So "a tan from a
peptide" is not a story about a new signal — it is a story about taking the
body's deliberately-feeble signal and making it relentless. That reframing is
what these three pieces teach, and it reinforces every cautionary page from the
biology side: the harms are not a side effect of potency, they *are* the
potency, arriving at receptors the local signal was never meant to reach.

Commission only when a correspondent can source the copy; each claim below
needs a primary citation (the pharmacology here is editorial recall, not
sourced in the audit). Each ships through the normal frontmatter/PR flow,
obeys every non-negotiable (physiology only, no compound to dose or source),
and forward-links from the predecessors named.

The entity page the cluster lacks. Target the "what is α-MSH / alpha-MSH /
melanocyte-stimulating hormone" query family head-on (serpTitle candidate:
"What is α-MSH? Alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone, explained").

The teaching insight: **the name misleads, and the potency gap is why.**
"Melanocyte-stimulating hormone" was named for what pituitary extract did to
frog and lizard skin in the assay era — a dramatic, visible pigment response
in cold-blooded skin. In adult humans the same peptide behaves less like a
circulating hormone and more like a *local* message: keratinocytes and
melanocytes make α-MSH on the spot after UV damage (POMC cleavage, driven by
p53), it acts over microns, and plasma enzymes clear it within minutes. It is
a 13-amino-acid linear peptide built around the His-Phe-Arg-Trp message
sequence — the pharmacophore every Melanotan amplified. The body keeps this
signal weak and brief *on purpose*; that is the control system, not a
limitation to be engineered away. Set up the potency frame the whole cluster
pays off: a natural agonist this transient is why a super-potent, protease-
resistant analog does "everything at once" — same keyhole, incomparable key.
Keep receptor-signalling detail light (owned by `one-hormone-five-receptors`,
which should forward-link) and the analog chemistry light (owned by
`before-the-receptor`). This is the hub: retrofit the ~15 passing α-MSH
mentions to link here instead of re-defining the hormone each time.

## Next wave — the SPF tier list (brief, 19 Nov 2026)

Working headline, deliberately a joke: **"The SPF tier list, ranked by how fast people betray them (SPF 100 is dead last)."** Commission as a `culture` essay. The ranking is a teaching device about feedback speed, not a protection ranking and not a shopping list. If a correspondent cannot hold both of those in the same piece, change the headline.

The teaching insight: **a sunscreen number is a teacher, and the higher the number, the slower the lesson.** People do not betray a bottle by choosing a low factor. They betray it the way the site already describes sunscreen failing — thin film, missed ears and scalp, no reapplication after water, a longer afternoon because the skin has not complained yet. Erythema is the only grade most people ever get. A modest factor hands that grade back the same day, so the habit can be corrected before the holiday is over. SPF 100 suppresses the grade. The user goes home unburned, decides the bottle worked, and repeats the thin coat. Dead last means last to be found out, not least protective.

What the piece has to say out loud, or it does not survive a dermatologist:

- Labeled SPF is a laboratory multiplier measured at 2 mg/cm². Everyday coats are much thinner, and protection does not fall in proportion to how little was put on, so the number on the front is not the number on the skin. Keep this conceptual; no application tutorial and no "use X pumps" arithmetic.
- The filtered-UVB curve is flat at the top. SPF 15, 30, 50, and 100 are separated by a few percentage points of UVB, not by a different kind of product. The jump from 50 to 100 is about one more percent of UVB blocked. That percent is real.
- Say the counter-result in the same breath: Williams et al., *JAAD* 2018, a randomized split-face study in actual outdoor use, found SPF 100+ sunscreens produced less sunburn than SPF 50+. The tier list does not claim high SPF protects less. It claims high SPF is a worse informant.
- The behavioral half is Autier et al., *J Natl Cancer Inst* 1999: in a double-blind trial, people given the higher factor stayed in the sun longer. That is the betrayal the ranking is measuring — duration and skipped reapplication, not the factor itself. FDA's February 2019 proposed sunscreen rule is the regulatory version of the same worry (high labeled values, small incremental benefit, a number that can be read as permission). Quote the proposal; do not narrate it as a final ban on SPF 100.
