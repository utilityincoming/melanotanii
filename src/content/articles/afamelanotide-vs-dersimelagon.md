Two legitimate drugs sit on the approved and pre-approval side of the
melanocortin record: **afamelanotide (Scenesse), an approved peptide implant,
and dersimelagon (MT-7117), an investigational oral small molecule.** Both
activate the melanocortin-1 receptor (MC1R) to increase pain-free sunlight
tolerance in rare genetic photodermatoses. Neither is a cosmetic tan.

## Why the delivery route differs

Peptides break down in the digestive tract, which is why [oral Melanotan II
capsules and gummies cannot work](/articles/the-pill-that-wasnt/) and why
afamelanotide ships as a clinic-placed subcutaneous implant. Dersimelagon
solves the oral problem by not being a peptide at all: it is a small molecule
designed to activate MC1R after swallowing, tested in the 165-patient INSPIRE
Phase 3 trial ([Tanabe Pharma, Jan 2026](/record/2026-01-20-tanabe-inspire/)).

## What neither drug is

Both programs sit inside the rare-disease ladder described in [the beachhead
indications](/articles/the-beachhead-indications/) and [the pipeline right
now](/articles/the-pipeline-right-now/). [Scenesse](/articles/scenesse/) is
restricted to accredited clinics for EPP, and
[dersimelagon](/articles/dersimelagon/) is not yet approved for any use.
Neither is an over-the-counter tanning pill or an alternative name for
gray-market Melanotan II.
