Melanotan II holds an unusual distinction: it may be the most widely
self-administered injectable drug never approved by any regulator on Earth.
Understanding how that happened is a case study in what fills the space when
legitimate development stalls and demand doesn't.

## How the market formed

The compound's structure was published in scientific literature and patents,
which meant any capable peptide synthesizer could make it. By the mid-2000s,
"research chemical" websites were selling vials labeled *not for human
consumption* — a legal fig leaf everyone involved understood as fiction.
Bodybuilding and tanning forums supplied the rest: crowd-sourced injection
protocols, trip reports, and reassurance, all from anonymous users with no
accountability for outcomes.

Media coverage dubbed it "the Barbie drug" for its advertised effect
profile — tanning, appetite suppression, and libido effects in one vial.
Health agencies in the UK, Australia, Denmark, Norway and elsewhere issued
public warnings and moved to block sales. The warnings documented the
problem; they did not end the market. What those agencies actually did next
is [the enforcement file](/articles/what-the-warnings-actually-did/).

## What the cautionary tale actually consists of

The case against gray-market Melanotan II doesn't require exaggeration. The
documented concerns are enough:

**Nobody verifies what's in the vial.** Analyses of seized and purchased
products have found variable purity, inconsistent actual peptide content,
and contamination risk inherent to unregulated manufacture of an injectable.
Whatever the true pharmacology of pure MT-II, gray-market users aren't
reliably getting pure MT-II.

**The mole problem is real.** Dermatology literature contains repeated case
reports of users developing darkened, changed moles — and the deeper worry
is diagnostic: a compound that darkens pigmented lesions can make early
melanoma harder to spot, which is a bitter irony for a molecule invented to
prevent melanoma. Case reports of melanoma in users exist; causation is
genuinely unresolved, and honest coverage says exactly that. Unresolved is
not reassuring.

**No safety data at the doses and durations people actually use.** The
formal human trials were small, short, and decades old. Everything beyond
them — years-long use, stacked compounds, forum dosing — is uncontrolled
experimentation with no one collecting the results.

## The part most coverage misses

The standard warning article stops at "don't inject mystery vials," which is
correct but incomplete. The fuller lesson is structural: demand for
photoprotection and sunless tanning was real and large, the approved pathway
never delivered a product for the general public, and an unregulated market
formed in the vacuum. Moralizing at individual users doesn't change that
dynamic. A legitimate, tested, approved alternative would — which is why the
final section of this site takes the future seriously instead of ending on
the warning.
