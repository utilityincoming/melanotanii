The names are one letter apart. The molecules are worlds apart, and the
difference matters, because people searching for one keep landing on the
other.

**Melatonin** is a hormone the pineal gland makes when it gets dark. It
is a small molecule built from tryptophan, sold over the counter in
nearly every country as a sleep supplement, and studied for decades. It
works through melatonin receptors, which pharmacologists abbreviate MT1
and MT2.

**Melanotan** — Melanotan I and Melanotan II — are synthetic peptides
modeled on α-MSH, the body's pigment hormone. Melanotan II is a ring of
seven amino acids, not approved by any regulator for any use, sold on
the gray market as an injection or nasal spray for tanning. It works
through the [melanocortin receptor
family](/articles/one-hormone-five-receptors/) — MC1R through MC5R —
which is why one compound can darken skin, turn a stomach, and light up
appetite and arousal circuits at the same time.

One is a sleep signal. The other is a five-receptor shout. They share a
spelling, nothing else.

## The abbreviation trap

The confusion has a technical trap built into it. Melanotan II is known
on the forums as "MT-II." The melatonin receptor subtypes are known in
the literature as MT1 and MT2. The same abbreviation, in two different
literatures, means two unrelated things. A curious reader who types MT-II
into a search engine gets sleep pharmacology and gray-market tanning
protocols on the same page of results.

That collision is not the market's fault, but the market has never
corrected it. A name that sounds like a cousin of a harmless, ubiquitous
supplement is a marketing asset. "Melanotan" inherits, by typography, a
fraction of melatonin's safety reputation — a supplement you can buy at a
pharmacy shelf — while carrying none of its testing history.

## How to keep them straight

The regulatory status is the cleanest tell. Melatonin is a legal,
labelled, dosed product wherever it is sold. Melanotan II is approved
nowhere, for anything — it is the subject of warnings from the FDA, TGA,
MHRA, and HPRA, and in Australia possession itself is a Schedule 9
offence.

If you arrived at this site looking for the sleep hormone, the answer is
short: it is a different molecule, and the similar name is doing work on
you. If you arrived looking for the tanning peptide, the rest of this
publication is the file on what that search leads to — starting with
