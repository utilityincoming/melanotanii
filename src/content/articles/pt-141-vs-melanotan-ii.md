The search that pairs these two names is usually looking for a substitute.
There isn't one. **PT-141 is the old code for an approved prescription drug.
Melanotan II is an unapproved peptide.** They came out of the same research
line. They are not interchangeable, and this page will not tell you how to
obtain either.

## What PT-141 actually is

PT-141 was the research name. The drug is bremelanotide, sold as Vyleesi.
The FDA approved it on 21 June 2019 for acquired, generalized hypoactive
sexual desire disorder in premenopausal women — low sexual desire that
causes marked distress, in that population, on a prescription.
([Vyleesi prescribing information, 2019](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2019/210557s000lbl.pdf);
[Dhillon and Keam, *Drugs*, 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31429064/).)
It is a melanocortin-receptor agonist aimed at the central circuits of
sexual response, not at a tan. The label is one sentence long in the way
that matters: one population, one disorder, a manufacturer, a regulator.
This page does not reprint a dose. A dose is not what the query is asking,
and it is not what distinguishes the two.

The origin is already told. A self-experiment with Melanotan II produced an
arousal effect the tanning program had not been built to find, and Palatin
developed that effect into bremelanotide rather than into a tan.
([The accident](/articles/the-accident-inside-melanotan/).)
That is history. It is not a reason to treat the parent peptide as the
child drug.

## What a listing called PT-141 usually is

The approved drug has a brand, a label, and a pharmacy. The code PT-141
also circulates on the research-chemical market, next to Melanotan II,
BPC-157, and the rest of the peptide menu. LegitScript's 2025 dataset,
cited in [how it's sold now](/articles/how-its-sold-now/), named that menu
as one trade. A page that says "not for human consumption" and then sells
a vial under a retired research code is not dispensing Vyleesi. It is
wearing the costume.

The costume does not make the contents safe, identical, or legal to sell
for the use the searcher has in mind. Australia's assays of Melanotan
products already showed that a label on this market is not a specification.
([What's in the vial](/articles/whats-in-the-vial/).)
There is no reason to assume a bottle marked PT-141 is held to a standard
the Melanotan bottles were not.

## What Melanotan II is not

Melanotan II has never been approved for sexual desire, for tanning, or for
anything else. It is not a cheaper Vyleesi. It is not "the same peptide,
basically." Using an unapproved compound because it shares an ancestor with
an approved one is how the gray market launders a substitution. This site
does not publish sourcing, suppliers, or usage guidance for that
substitution — not as comparison, not as harm reduction, not as history.

The approved drug is the one with the label. The unapproved peptide is the
[cautionary tale](/articles/barbie-drug-gray-market/). If a future
melanocortin medicine earns a wider label, it will earn it in a trial, not
in a checkout.
