If you searched Scenesse hoping for a legal tan, the answer is no. **Scenesse
is an approved medicine for a rare disease in which light causes pain.** It
is not a cosmetic. It is not a vial. It is not Melanotan II with a better
name.

## What the approvals actually say

Scenesse is the brand name of afamelanotide, a melanocortin-1 receptor
agonist. The European Commission granted a marketing authorisation for it
on 22 December 2014, for erythropoietic protoporphyria.
([EMA EPAR](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/scenesse).)
The FDA's 2019 label indicates it to increase pain-free light exposure in
adults with a history of phototoxic reactions from that same disease.
([Prescribing information, revised October 2019](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2019/210797s000lbl.pdf).)
Canada followed in 2026; that date is on the [regulatory file](/regulatory/).

The label is the lock. The endpoint in the trials was time in light without
pain, in people whose skin burns in minutes because of a rare metabolic
disorder. It was not hours of cosmetic darkness in healthy skin, and it was
not skin cancer prevented. The [prevention-claim file](/articles/the-prevention-claim-misread/)
is where that misreading is taken apart.

It is also not a product a person inserts at home. The U.S. label says a
healthcare professional who has completed the manufacturer's training
administers the implant. That sentence is here so the object is clear. It
is not instructions.

## Why you still cannot have it for a tan

The molecule cleared a regulator. The indication did not widen to the use
the gray market wants. Getting from "treats a rare light-pain disease" to
"darkens healthy skin, safely, for anyone" is a trials-and-payer problem,
not a chemistry problem. That argument is the [middle-ground essay](/articles/middle-ground-agonist-already-exists/).
This page exists so the query "Scenesse tan" lands on the refusal before it
lands on the economics.

Afamelanotide is the developed form of Melanotan I, the linear peptide from
the Arizona program. Melanotan II — the cyclic peptide in the unlabeled
vials — was the sibling that was not developed into a medicine.
([One number, two fates](/articles/melanotan-1-vs-melanotan-2/).)
Sharing a laboratory is not sharing a supply chain. A clinic implant with a
named manufacturer is not [a vial nobody tested](/articles/whats-in-the-vial/).

Clinuvel is studying the same molecule in other diseases, including
vitiligo. A repigmentation trial in people who have lost pigment is not a
tanning indication either. That misreading is the one
[the beachhead file](/articles/the-beachhead-indications/) already draws.
The readout itself gets a page when the result is public, not before.

*Hope that a tested photoprotective medicine eventually reaches ordinary
skin is this site's labeled opinion. Scenesse, today, is not that medicine.
It is a rare-disease implant. Anything sold as the tan Scenesse couldn't
be is the [cautionary tale](/articles/barbie-drug-gray-market/).*
