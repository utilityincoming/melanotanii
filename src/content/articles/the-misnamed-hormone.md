The hormone at the centre of this whole story is misnamed, and the name is
worth taking apart, because almost everything the gray market gets wrong
about it follows from believing the label.

"Melanocyte-stimulating hormone" was christened for a party trick. In the
1950s, when Aaron Lerner and his colleagues isolated the peptide from
pituitary glands, the way you measured it was to drop it onto a piece of
frog or lizard skin and watch the skin darken — pigment granules fanning out
across the melanophores within minutes. It was a dramatic, visible, quantifiable
response, and it gave the molecule its name. The name records what pituitary
extract did to a cold-blooded animal on a lab bench. It does not describe what
the peptide is to you.

## What it actually is

α-MSH is a short peptide — thirteen amino acids, written
Ac-Ser-Tyr-Ser-Met-Glu-His-Phe-Arg-Trp-Gly-Lys-Pro-Val-NH₂, with a chemically
capped head and tail. Buried in the middle is the four-residue stretch
His-Phe-Arg-Trp, the "message sequence": the minimal core that the receptor
actually reads. Everything the [Arizona chemists did](/articles/before-the-receptor/)
a generation later was an attempt to amplify and stabilise that message. It is
the pharmacophore every Melanotan was cut to fit.

The body does not keep α-MSH in a gland and release it into the bloodstream
like a classic hormone. It makes it on the spot. α-MSH is one of several
fragments cut from a much larger precursor protein, pro-opiomelanocortin, or
POMC — the same precursor that yields ACTH and β-endorphin. Different tissues
cut POMC differently, so the same parent protein becomes a stress hormone in
one place and a pigment signal in another.

## Where it comes from, and when

In skin, the trigger is damage. Ultraviolet light breaks DNA in
keratinocytes — the structural cells of the epidermis — and that damage
stabilises the p53 protein, the cell's emergency switch. p53 binds the POMC
gene and turns it on. The keratinocyte cuts α-MSH from the POMC it has just
made and releases it to the pigment cells sitting next to it. Those
melanocytes carry [MC1R](/articles/why-redheads-burn-mc1r/) on their surface;
α-MSH binds it, cAMP rises inside the cell, the master pigment regulator MITF
is switched up, and the cell starts manufacturing eumelanin — the brown-black
pigment that absorbs UV. That is a tan: a [wound response](/articles/the-tan-without-the-sun/),
pigment made only after the harm that called for it.

## The two things the name hides

Read that pathway again and two features stand out, neither of them in the
word "hormone."

The first is that α-MSH is **local**. It is released microns from where it
acts, into the gap between a keratinocyte and the pigment cell beside it. It
is not a courier dispatched through the bloodstream to the whole body.

The second is that it is **brief**. Plasma enzymes degrade α-MSH within
minutes. The signal is designed to be weak and fleeting — switched on by
damage, read by the cell next door, and erased almost immediately.

Those are not shortcomings. They are the control system. The body tunes
pigment with a signal it can start and stop fast, in one small patch of skin,
because unbounded pigment-making everywhere, all the time, is not something a
body wants. The feebleness is the feature.

## Why this is the whole story

Hold that picture next to what the gray market injects, and the gap is the
entire point. The lab did not discover a new signal; it took the body's
deliberately-feeble one and rebuilt it to be relentless. The
[two amino-acid swaps](/articles/before-the-receptor/) that made
Melanotan's superpotent ancestor — norleucine for the oxidation-prone
methionine, a D-form phenylalanine the enzymes can't cut — exist precisely to
defeat the "cleared in minutes" design. Cyclisation and injection defeat the
"made where it's needed" design. What is left is the same message sequence,
reading the same receptor, but [now circulating for hours and reaching every
melanocortin receptor in the body](/articles/a-drug-with-no-address/) — the
appetite ones, the arousal ones, the ones in the brain.

That is why a single peptide [tans, sickens, and arouses at
once](/articles/one-hormone-five-receptors/). It is not a mysterious property
of the molecule. It is what happens when you take a whisper the body meant to
last a moment in one spot and turn it into a shout that lasts all day
everywhere. When the market says Melanotan is "[just a hormone your body
makes](/articles/the-natural-fallacy/)," the honest translation is: it is
patterned on one, and engineered against every safeguard that made the
original safe to have.

And pigment, finally, is only one of α-MSH's jobs. The same peptide family is
woven into inflammation, energy balance, and more — which is another way of
saying the body never built this signal to be a tanning switch you could leave
on. It built a brief, local message and gave it a name, seventy years ago, for
the one thing it did to a frog.
