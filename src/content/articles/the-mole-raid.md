faq:
  - q: "Do Melanotan mole changes fade after stopping?"
    a: "In the earliest reported case — a patient with a history of melanoma who erupted new nevi on an α-MSH product — the changes faded after the product was stopped, and the report is one of the few that documents the fade. But biopsies taken while the changes were present showed atypia, fading is not documented for every case, and no published rule tells a person which darkening mole is drug effect and which is something else. A lesion that is changing is a lesion for a dermatologist to assess, not to wait out."
  - q: "How many melanoma cases have been published in melanotan users?"
    a: "The peer-reviewed corpus carries at least five melanoma diagnoses: an invasive melanoma in the British Journal of Dermatology (2011), a melanoma in situ in the Australasian Journal of Dermatology (2012), a melanoma in Dermatology (2014), an oral mucosal melanoma questioned after a nasal spray (2025), and five primary melanomas in situ in a single patient with combined exposures (2026). Case reports raise an alarm; they cannot prove causation. Australia's FOI-released adverse-event file adds two melanoma reports among 89 spontaneous reports, with causality rated 'possible'."
  - q: "Why do case reports keep coming if nothing is proven?"
    a: "Because the exposure keeps happening and the only publication channel that exists for it is the case report. Melanotan II has no trial programme, no market authorisation and no manufacturer pharmacovigilance anywhere it is sold for tanning, so harms surface one patient at a time — written up by whichever dermatologist saw them. Meanwhile the market grew: a December 2025 monitoring report found peptide advertising up 308% year-over-year, with melanotan first among the most observed product names."
  - q: "Can mole mapping still catch a melanoma in someone who has used melanotan?"
    a: "Mole mapping, total-body photography and the ugly-duckling sign all compare present skin to a stable past. A drug that darkens existing moles and erupts new ones moves that baseline for the duration of exposure — one 2012 report documented dermoscopic changes during use that made it difficult to differentiate a naevus from a melanoma. The tools still work in professional hands; what they need from the patient is honesty. A dermatologist told the product name, the exposure dates and when changes were noticed can put the comparison back into context. The published literature contains no self-assessment shortcut, and this site will not invent one."

[The Mole Problem](/articles/the-mole-problem/) made the argument: a drug
whose defining action is darkening and multiplying moles degrades the
early-warning system that makes melanoma survivable, whether or not it ever
causes a cancer. This piece opens the file behind that argument — every
peer-reviewed melanotan mole report this site could verify, read in order,
and read against the other literature, the science of watching moles.

The word *raid* is deliberate. Dermatologists do not merely look at moles.
They map them — the ugly duckling that differs from its neighbours, the
serial dermoscopy compared to the last visit, the total-body photograph
compared to an accurate past self. Melanotan II raids the map: it darkens
the landmarks, plants new ones, and redraws the territory mid-survey.

## 1996: darkening was the endpoint

The first clinical record of any of this is the pilot phase-I study of
Melanotan II published in *Life Sciences* in 1996
([PMID 8637402](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8637402/)) — three healthy
men, skin darkening observed, the pigmentary effect read as evidence the
compound worked. For the drug's developers, a darkening mole was not a
surveillance problem. It was pharmacology.

Nothing in that record anticipates what came later, and nothing in it needed
to: the pilot ran on volunteers without the thing that makes mole change
dangerous — a mole map worth preserving. The problem arrives when the same
molecule reaches the population dermatologists survey.

## 2009: two papers flip the meaning

Thirteen years later, two reports land in the same year and change what the
pigment means.

In the *British Journal of Dermatology*, eruptive melanocytic naevi
following melanotan injection
([PMID 19575725](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19575725/)): new moles
appearing in crops, in a person who had bought the peptide to tan.

In *Archives of Dermatology*, the heavier one — α-melanocyte-stimulating
hormone-induced eruptive nevi
([PMID 19380666](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19380666/)). The patient
carried a personal history of melanoma and multiple dysplastic nevi. After
exposure to an α-MSH product, new nevi erupted and existing ones darkened
and enlarged; biopsies taken while the changes were present showed atypia.
The changes faded after the product was stopped — one of the very few
documented fades in the whole corpus. The report's significance is the
population it happened in: a melanocortin driving melanocyte proliferation
in exactly the patient for whom proliferation is the disease. The authors
said as much, and the literature has never un-said it.

Both papers were reading the same receptor agonism the 1996 pilot called
efficacy. From 2009 onward, in the dermatology literature, it is also a
hazard.

## 2010–2014: the corpus accumulates

What follows is not one dramatic case. It is five years of steady
publication, in six countries, in five languages — international because the
market was international.

- **2010.** *BJD* commentary: melanotropic peptides, "more than just 'Barbie
  drugs' and 'sun-tan jabs'?"
  ([PMID 20545686](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20545686/)) — the field
  begins treating the peptides as a dermatology problem in their own right.
- **2011.** *BJD*: melanotan-associated melanoma
  ([PMID 21564053](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21564053/)). The corpus's
  first published melanoma.
- **2012.** The densest year. *Australasian Journal of Dermatology*:
  melanotan-associated melanoma in situ
  ([PMID 22724573](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22724573/)). *Actas
  Dermo-Sifiliográficas* (Spanish): eruptive dysplastic nevi after melanotan
  use ([PMID 22425244](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22425244/)).
  *Dermatology Practical & Conceptual*: a teenager with FAMMM syndrome —
  familial atypical multiple mole–melanoma syndrome, a germline
  predisposition — whose melanocytic lesions changed after melanotan
  injections paired with sunbed use
  ([PMID 23785612](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23785612/)). And *Der
  Hautarzt* (German): videodermoscopic changes in melanocytic nevi *during*
  melanotan II use ([PMID 23052015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23052015/))
  — the one report that watched the monitoring instrument itself meet the
  drug, and recorded changes that made it difficult to tell a naevus from a
  melanoma.
- **2013.** *Irish Medical Journal*: atypical melanocytic naevi following
  melanotan injection ([PMID 23914578](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23914578/)).
  *Läkartidningen* (Swedish): eruptive nevi after injections of drugs
  marketed as melanotan II — Sweden's first two described cases
  ([PMID 23451671](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23451671/)).
- **2014.** *European Journal of Dermatology*: eruptive naevi and darkening
  of pre-existing naevi **24 hours after a single injection**
  ([PMID 24334249](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24334249/)) — the corpus's
  fastest timeline, one dose to a redrawn map in a day. *Dermatology*
  (Basel): melanoma associated with melanotan-II use
  ([PMID 24355990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24355990/)). And *JAAD*'s
  tanning update files the peptides under tanning risk proper
  ([PMID 24388421](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24388421/)).

Read in order, the pattern is not a scatter of anecdotes. It is a
consistency: different countries, different journals, different products
bought from different gray markets, the same two findings — moles arrive in
crops, and moles already there darken, enlarge, and sometimes come back
atypical on biopsy.

## The other literature: what watching moles assumes

The corpus only means what it means next to the science it disrupts. Four
tools carry melanoma surveillance, and each rests on an assumption the case
reports attack directly.

The **ugly-duckling sign** — the mole that differs from its neighbours is the
one to biopsy. *Archives of Dermatology* tested how well observers agree on
it ([PMID 18209169](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18209169/)): agreement
depends on the outlier standing out against a stable field.

**Serial mole mapping** — digital dermoscopy compared visit to visit.
*Dermatologic Clinics* laid out the method
([PMID 28886799](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28886799/)): management
works by comparison against a baseline that holds between appointments.

**Total-body photography** — the whole skin surface archived and re-read. The
2021 systematic review of its value for early melanoma detection
([PMID 33578996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33578996/)) rests on the
photograph being an accurate past self.

And the reviews that classify what the corpus is: drug-induced eruptive
melanocytic nevi are a recognised category, melanotan among the causes
([PMID 31119650](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31119650/)), and the
unregulated α-MSH analogues carry a published risk literature of their own
([PMID 28266027](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28266027/)).

## 2025–2026: the file is still growing

A corpus that ended in 2014 could be filed as history. This one did not.

- **2025.** *International Journal of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery* asks
  whether a Melanotan II nasal spray is a possible risk factor for oral
  mucosal malignant melanoma
  ([PMID 40210573](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40210573/)) — the route
  extended the question into the mouth (the dedicated record:
  [The Mouth Is in the Record](/articles/the-mouth-is-in-the-record/)).
- **2026.** *JAAD Case Reports*: **five primary melanomas in situ** in a
  single patient with recent tanning-bed use, melanotan exposure and
  anabolic hormone use
  ([PMID 42328529](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42328529/)) — the corpus's
  densest single patient, and a reminder that the exposures arrive stacked,
  which is exactly why no case report can carry causation alone.
- **2026.** *JAAD Case Reports* again, on the delivery mechanism itself: the
  dangers of social-media misinformation around Melanotan II
  ([PMID 43060386](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43060386/)). A decade
  after the corpus began, the exposure vector is a feed. The market data say
  the same thing from the other side — LegitScript's December 2025 release
  found problematic-peptide advertising up 308% year-over-year, melanotan
  first among the most observed product names
  ([the record](/record/2025-12-09-legitscript/)).

And whatever the surveillance literature assumes about what a user is
applying, the product itself does not keep its promises: the TGA's August
2026 assay of seized "30 MG" nasal-spray bottles found 22 mg to 54 mg
([the record](/record/2026-08-17-tga-dosing-alert/)). The dose on the label
is decoration.

## What the corpus says — and what it can't

Twenty-plus publications. Seventeen years of case reports after a pilot that
called darkening success. Six countries, five languages, journals from the
*BJD* and *Archives of Dermatology* to *Läkartidningen*. Two findings hold
across all of it:

**Moles change.** New nevi erupt in crops; existing nevi darken and enlarge;
the timeline runs from months to a single day; some biopsied lesions came
back atypical. This is not disputed anywhere in the literature.

**In some reports, the change reaches melanoma.** At least five diagnoses
across the corpus — 2011, 2012, 2014, 2025's oral question, 2026's five in
situ — plus two melanoma reports inside Australia's FOI-released
adverse-event file, causality rated "possible"
([the record](/record/2026-07-06-foi-26-3029/)).

What the corpus cannot say is *caused*. Case reports raise alarms; they
don't prove them. The exposures stack — sunbeds, anabolic hormones, prior
melanoma, germline syndromes — and there is no denominator: no trial, no
authorisation, no manufacturer pharmacovigilance, anywhere melanotan is sold
for tanning. That is what the Australian FOI file shows from the regulator's
side: 89 spontaneous reports in four years for a substance with no safety
infrastructure at all, released only because someone filed a request. No RCT
will ever close the question either — nobody should run a tanning peptide
trial in melanoma-prone volunteers, and the fact that the question can only
ever be answered by accidents of exposure is itself the indictment.

What the corpus *does* establish needs no causation at all. Every tool built
to catch melanoma early — the ugly duckling, the E, the serial map, the
photograph — runs on a stable baseline, and this drug's defining action is
to move the baseline. The tan fades; the map has to be redrawn; and the
literature, across thirty years and twenty-plus publications, has never
produced a rule for telling which changed mole was the drug and which was
the disease. That distinction has only ever been made one way: by a
dermatologist, with a dermoscope, and where indicated a biopsy.

The argument in one page is [The Mole Problem](/articles/the-mole-problem/).
The cases people send photographs of are in the [skin visual
record](/skin-record/). The rest of what the vial does is in the [side-effect
ledger](/articles/the-side-effect-ledger/). This page is the file itself —
and the file is still open.
