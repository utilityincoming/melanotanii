The gray market has begun selling Melanotan II in forms the needle era
never had: capsules, gummies, flavored drinks. They are the friendliest
costume yet. They are also, as a delivery of this molecule, a chemical
impossibility.

Melanotan II is a cyclic peptide — seven amino acids locked into a ring.
That ring was engineered in the 1980s precisely so the molecule could
survive long enough, *once injected*, to outlast the body's own α-MSH,
which is destroyed within minutes. The digestive tract is a different
problem from plasma enzymes. A swallowed peptide meets stomach acid and a
battery of proteases whose entire job is dismantling proteins into single
amino acids before they cross the gut wall. Cyclicity slows the process.
It does not stop it, and nothing about this molecule crosses the gut wall
intact in meaningful quantities.

Every approved melanocortin drug confirms the point by example.
Afamelanotide is a subcutaneous implant. Bremelanotide is an
auto-injector. Setmelanotide is a daily injection. Three companies,
holding the patents, with every commercial incentive to sell a pill,
sell needles. The wellness-menu vendors selling a capsule have no
patent, no lab, and no incentive to tell you why.

## What an oral product actually is

That leaves two possibilities, and neither is an oral tanning peptide.

The product contains no Melanotan II at all. Any colour change it
produces comes from something else — and the history of that something
else has its own ending. Canthaxanthin "tanning pills" were sold for
decades before the FDA made its position plain: the colour additive is
approved for food, not for tanning, and the pills have been linked to
canthaxanthin retinopathy — crystals in the eye — and liver problems.
Dyes colour the skin from the inside. They are not a tan, and they are
not melanin.

Or the product does contain Melanotan II — in which case it is paying
for a molecule that will be digested before it can reach a single
receptor, and whatever effect the buyer notices is not the peptide.

The [vial problem](/articles/whats-in-the-vial/) applies at both exits.
A capsule from an unknown vendor is an assumption inside a gelatin shell:
no pharmacopeia, no label verification, no regulator who has ever seen
the batch.

The market sells the pill because the pill is what a nervous buyer wants
to exist — the tan without the needle, the needle without the decision.
The chemistry doesn't care what the buyer wants. An oral Melanotan
product that appears to work is the proof that it isn't one.
