*Analysis — the future-agonist thesis is opinion, labeled as such. This file is
written to be read the week the data lands.*

For months the loudest marker on this site's [pipeline snapshot](/articles/the-pipeline-right-now/)
has been a date, not a result: the Phase III vitiligo readout for **afamelanotide**
(Scenesse) — the CUV105 program — due around the end of 2026. It is, on any honest
accounting, the biggest melanocortin-agonist data event of the year. And the moment
it lands, it will be misread.

The misreading is predictable because it is the same one the gray market has made
for eighteen years: *a drug that adds pigment to skin is a tanning drug.* This page
exists to answer that in advance — to say what a vitiligo result can and cannot
settle, before the caption writers get to it.

## What the trial actually measures

Non-segmental vitiligo is an autoimmune disease. The immune system destroys
melanocytes, and skin loses its colour in expanding patches. The clinical question
in a vitiligo trial is narrow and specific: in people who have *lost* pigment, can
driving the melanocortin-1 receptor — the [same receptor](/articles/one-hormone-five-receptors/)
at the centre of the tanning signal — bring measurable colour *back*?

That is a repigmentation endpoint, scored on affected skin in diagnosed patients.
It is not a sun-damage-prevention endpoint. It is not a measure of anything that
happens in the skin of a healthy person who simply wants to be darker. The two
share a pigment pathway and nothing else — the same way a drug that regrows hair in
alopecia is not a styling product.

The design makes the point sharper still. CUV105 is a randomised Phase III study
run across 37 sites on three continents, enrolling more than 200 patients with
darker skin types (Fitzpatrick III–VI), aged 12 and up. Crucially, it does not test
afamelanotide as a standalone tanning agent: it tests the drug *plus* narrowband
UVB phototherapy against narrowband UVB alone — the drug as an add-on to the
standard of care for a disease, not a cosmetic on its own. The primary endpoint is
**T-VASI50**: the proportion of patients whose total-body vitiligo area improves by
at least 50% on the Vitiligo Area Scoring Index. A key secondary, **F-VASI75**, asks
the same of the face at a 75% threshold. Treatment runs 20 weeks, with six months of
follow-up. Afamelanotide is not approved for vitiligo anywhere; the indication
depends entirely on this result and the one after it.

## Why "it repigments skin" is not "it's a tanning drug"

Three gaps separate a vitiligo result from the cosmetic claim the market wants, and
every one of them survives a positive readout.

- **A disease population, not the public.** The benefit-risk arithmetic that
  justifies a melanocortin agonist in vitiligo is the arithmetic of a disabling
  autoimmune condition with few good options. None of that math transfers to a
  healthy person seeking a cosmetic tan, where the risk sits on one side of the
  ledger and vanity on the other. This is the exact pattern the
  [beachhead file](/articles/the-beachhead-indications/) describes: each approved
  indication is narrow, reimbursable, and defended by a specific disease — a rung,
  not the roof.

- **Repigmentation is not photoprotection.** Restoring colour to depigmented skin
  and *protecting* skin from UV damage are different claims with different
  endpoints and different trials. As the
  [prevention-claim file](/articles/the-prevention-claim-misread/) lays out,
  nobody has run the large, long cancer- or damage-endpoint study a
  "protects you from the sun" claim would require. A vitiligo win does not start
  that trial; it does not even point at it.

- **The delivery format is still the clinic.** Afamelanotide is a controlled,
  clinic-administered implant, dosed and monitored under supervision. That is the
  opposite of a vial bought from a website and injected at home. A positive trial
  is evidence *for the regulated product*, and if anything it is evidence against
  the counterfeit — because it shows what a real melanocortin indication looks
  like, and how little the gray market resembles one.

## The honest reading, either way

If CUV105 reads out positive, the right conclusion is the disciplined one this
[Next Agonist](/articles/middle-ground-agonist-already-exists/) thread keeps
returning to: the approved molecule climbed one more rung of the indication ladder.
That genuinely matters — each cleared indication widens what a regulator has seen
this receptor class safely do, and vitiligo is a larger, more visible disease than
the porphyrias that came before it. It is real progress toward a science arriving
*through the front door*. It is still not a tan you can buy.

If it reads out negative or mixed, nothing about the gray market changes either.
The unapproved vials were never riding on this endpoint; they were never riding on
any endpoint. The [cautionary tale](/articles/barbie-drug-gray-market/) is
indifferent to CUV105's result, because it was never evidence-led to begin with.

So the one caption to distrust, the week this lands, is the one that turns a
vitiligo repigmentation figure into a verdict on tanning. That figure answers a
question about restoring lost colour to diseased skin. The question the market keeps
asking — *is a safe tanning drug for everyone finally here?* — is answered on a
different page, by a trial [nobody is paying for](/articles/who-would-pay-for-the-trial/).
