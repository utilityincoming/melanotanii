You can see what a signal is for by watching bodies where it is stuck. No
peptide for sale is required. [α-MSH](/articles/the-misnamed-hormone/) and the
family it belongs to already run, in public, at both ends of their range —
too much upstream, and none at all. Both ends are diseases. That is the
lesson.

## Too much: the skin darkens when the precursor will not stop

α-MSH is one fragment of a larger protein, POMC. Another fragment is ACTH,
the pituitary hormone that tells the adrenal to make cortisol.

In primary adrenal insufficiency — Addison's disease — the adrenal does not
answer. Cortisol stays low, feedback fails, and the pituitary keeps releasing
ACTH. ACTH is itself a melanocortin peptide. At the concentrations this
disease produces, it activates MC1R, the same pigment receptor α-MSH uses.
Skin darkens: creases, scars, gums, sun-exposed skin, sometimes a new
photograph that no longer matches the old one. Those sites are drawn, not
photographed, in the [skin visual record](/skin-record/). Physicians have used that
darkening as a clue for generations. It is not a tan anyone ordered. It is
melanocortin drive, unopposed, writing itself on the skin.

The point for this site is narrow and worth being narrow about. Pigment can
be produced in a person by a melanocortin signal alone, without a week at the
beach. That is what the peptide market borrows when it says a vial is "just
turning on what the body already does." Addison's is the existence proof of
the mechanism and, in the same breath, the warning: the high state is an
illness. The darkening is not a benefit to be copied. Treating the adrenal
failure is a medical problem this page does not advise on.

## Too little: red hair, pale skin, and a receptor with nothing to read

Run the same axis the other way. Some children are born unable to make POMC
at all. The mutation, first described in people by Krude and colleagues in
1998, removes the precursor, so the fragments never appear: no ACTH for the
adrenal, no α-MSH for the pigment receptor, no melanocortin signal at the
hypothalamic receptor that restrains appetite.

The picture that follows is the cluster this site already describes, failing
from the source rather than from a broken lock. Red hair. Pale skin that
does not tan. Adrenal crisis if it is not recognised. Early, severe obesity.
[MC1R variants](/articles/why-redheads-burn-mc1r/) produce a similar pigment
story by building a receptor that answers poorly; POMC deficiency produces it
by never sending the message. Same axis, opposite fault. The genetics are
laid out further in
[why the tanning response is not universal](/articles/mc1r-variants-not-universal/).

That missing message is also why an MC4R agonist, setmelanotide (Imcivree),
exists as a prescription drug for a handful of ultra-rare genetic obesities,
POMC deficiency among them. The approved drug is named on the
[wellness-menu piece](/articles/the-wellness-menu/), which is where its
indication and its limits live. It is not a tanning product, and a disease
in which the signal is absent is not evidence that healthy people need more
of it.

## The brake that was part of the design

An accelerator with no brake is not how this system is built.

The endogenous antagonist at MC1R is agouti signalling protein, ASIP. In the
mouse, switching agouti on across the hair cycle bands the pigment from black
eumelanin to yellow pheomelanin — the same fork the redhead gene forces by
weakening the receptor. In people, ASIP is expressed in skin and is part of
how the melanocyte is told to stop. Lu and colleagues showed in 1994 that
agouti protein antagonises the melanocyte-stimulating hormone receptor: it
occupies the lock so the hormone cannot. The dial has two hands.

A gray-market compound built to be more agonist for longer throws that pair
away. It has no brake of its own, it resists the enzymes that were the
off-switch, and it does not stay local. The
[potency page](/articles/potent-superpotent-relentless/) separates the three
levers that produce that relentlessness. This page adds the human evidence
that relentlessness was never the setpoint. The body can darken skin with a
melanocortin signal. It can fail to darken skin when the signal is missing.
What it does not do, in health, is leave the accelerator down.

Physiology only. Nothing here is a treatment, a dose, or a reason to seek
either end of the dial.
