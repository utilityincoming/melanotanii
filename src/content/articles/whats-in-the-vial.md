Set aside, for a moment, every argument about what pure Melanotan II does in
the human body. There is a prior problem no gray-market user gets past, and it
makes the rest of the debate almost academic: they don't have pure Melanotan
II. They have whatever is in the vial.

## A product defined by not being a product

Melanotan II is sold as a "research chemical" — typically a freeze-dried
powder in a small glass vial, stamped *not for human consumption*, a
disclaimer everyone in the transaction treats as theater. Behind that vial
there is no pharmacopeia. No regulator sets a purity standard for it. No
inspector visits the facility. No batch is tested and released against a
specification before it ships. The label tells you nothing about the contents
because there is nothing standing behind the label to make it mean anything.

Every assurance a person has about a legitimate injectable medicine — that it
is what it says, in the amount it says, made cleanly — is simply absent here.

## Three unknowns, stacked

**Identity and purity.** Analyses of seized and test-purchased peptides
routinely turn up product that is under-strength, off-specification, or not
precisely the compound claimed. Peptide synthesis leaves behind truncated and
modified byproducts; with no assay, they come along for the ride. What
fraction of a given vial is the intended molecule is, to the buyer,
unknowable.

**Sterility.** This is an injection. Sterile manufacture of an injectable is a
demanding, heavily regulated discipline precisely because contamination
introduced this way bypasses every defense the skin provides and goes straight
into the body. An unregulated powder from an unnamed lab carries no sterility
assurance whatsoever.

**Dose.** The powder is reconstituted by the user, with liquid they add
themselves, and the amount is worked out by eye and arithmetic. Small errors
scale into large ones fast. The medical literature includes a man who injected
several times a typical starting amount and arrived at the emergency department
with sympathomimetic toxicity, rhabdomyolysis — muscle tissue breaking down —
and kidney injury. The compound category and the do-it-yourself format
practically manufacture that kind of mistake.

## Why this is the trump card

Notice what every other debate about Melanotan II quietly assumes: a known
quantity of a known substance. Is it safe? Does it work? At what exposure?
Every one of those questions presupposes you know what you took. The gray
market cannot supply that premise.

So whatever the true pharmacology of clean, correctly measured Melanotan II
eventually turns out to be — and this site takes that science seriously
elsewhere — it is not what is reliably coming out of an unlabeled vial
reconstituted on a kitchen counter. The uncertainty isn't a caveat attached
to the product. On a product whose entire definition is the absence of a
trustworthy label, the uncertainty *is* the product. That's the fact no dosing
chart or forum protocol can engineer away, and it belongs at the center of the
whole [side-effect ledger](/articles/the-side-effect-ledger/), because it
undermines every other line in it.

Why the protocols keep being written anyway — and why the math in them
cannot survive the label — is [the protocol
illusion](/articles/the-protocol-illusion/).
