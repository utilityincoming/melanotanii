Most drugs that reach people through back channels are treating something.
Pain that a clinic under-manages, anxiety, a disease the system prices out of
reach — the illicit supply tracks an unmet medical need. Melanotan II is
stranger than that, and the strangeness is the point. It went underground to
serve a *cosmetic* desire. A want, not a need. Once you see that distinction, most
of the gray market's peculiar shape falls into place.

## Why the front door was locked

The legitimate route to a mass-market tanning drug was never merely slow. It was,
commercially, a dead end — and for a specific reason.

Selling a tan as *protection* means making a prevention claim, and prevention
is the single hardest thing to prove in all of medicine. You have to
demonstrate that something *didn't* happen — a cancer that never appeared — in
very large numbers of people, followed for many years. That is
[the prevention-trial problem](/articles/the-prevention-trial-problem/), and it
is brutal arithmetic: no company was ever going to fund a decade-long,
tens-of-thousands-strong melanoma-prevention trial in order to sell a
cosmetic glow. The one melanocortin drug that did earn approval,
afamelanotide, only managed it by
[aiming at a rare disease instead of the general public](/articles/middle-ground-agonist-already-exists/) —
a narrow door, deliberately chosen, precisely because the wide one was shut.

So the approved pathway to a tanning drug for everyone was closed, not by an
oversight, but by the structure of what a tan actually is.

## Why the demand didn't wait

Locked front doors do nothing to reduce demand — they only redirect it. And the
demand here was vast. A [century of fashion](/articles/how-a-suntan-became-desirable/)
had made a tan feel close to necessary, and beneath the fashion,
[the sun had wired an actual reward into getting one](/articles/tanorexia-the-opioid-your-skin-makes/).
This was not a fringe appetite waiting for a nudge. It was mass-scale and, in
part, biologically underwritten.

When a want that large meets a legitimate supply of exactly zero, the space
fills from somewhere. And Melanotan II was unusually easy to supply: its
structure sat in the published literature and the patents, so
[any competent peptide lab could make it](/articles/how-the-science-escaped-the-lab/).
The vials arrived labelled *research chemical — not for human consumption*, a
disclaimer everyone involved read as the fiction it was, and the forums
supplied the rest: the protocols, the trip reports, the reassurance from
strangers with no stake in how it turned out.

## The asymmetry that makes it dangerous

Here is the part worth stating plainly, because it is where the "want, not a
need" framing stops being a tidy observation and starts mattering.

A *need*-driven gray market — think smuggled insulin, or generic HIV drugs
crossing a border — tends to recruit people who have weighed genuine stakes:
the risk of the unregulated supply against the risk of going without. The math,
however grim, is real on both sides.

A *want*-driven market has no such ballast. The upside on offer is a slightly
faster, slightly deeper tan. The downside is
[moles that change and a melanoma question no one can close](/articles/the-mole-problem/),
[a vial whose contents nobody has verified](/articles/whats-in-the-vial/), and
[the rest of a documented side-effect ledger](/articles/the-side-effect-ledger/).
That is a wildly lopsided trade — and the cosmetic framing is exactly what
hides the lopsidedness. A product that looks like a beauty treatment gets
judged like a beauty treatment, even as it behaves like an unregulated
injectable hormone. The mismatch between how it feels and what it is *is* the
hazard.

## What actually closes an underground

You cannot moralize a want out of existence, and prohibition on its own never
has — the warnings from health agencies were real, and the market outlived them.
The honest conclusion is the one this site keeps arriving at from every
direction: the durable fix for a gray market built on unmet desire is not a
louder warning. It is a legitimate, tested alternative good enough to make the
underground version pointless —
[the safe tanning drug that would have to exist](/articles/what-a-safe-tanning-drug-requires/)
for the demand to finally have somewhere honest to go.

Until that arrives, a cosmetic goes on living inside a pharmacology it was
never candid about — and, as the next piece shows, it has found a far larger
audience than the forums ever reached.
