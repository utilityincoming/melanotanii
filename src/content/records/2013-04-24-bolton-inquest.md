In September 2012, Jenna Wilson-Vickers, 26, was found collapsed in a
stand-up tanning cabinet at the Tantastic salon in Bolton, Lancashire, and
was declared dead at the scene. Bolton Coroner's Court heard she had been
injecting Melanotan II into her stomach for weeks — the vial in her bedroom
had been bought from a website called Totally Tanning — and had been using
sunbeds excessively; on the day she died she was having her third
12-minute session in three days.

The inquest jury, reported on 24 April 2013, found she died from heart
failure linked to obesity, not from the injections. The pathologist,
Dr Patrick Waugh, told the jury: "It remains that there is insufficient
information in respect of Melanotan II to allow any realistic assessment of
its significance in the death of Jenna. The possibility of adverse reaction
to Melanotan can't be excluded, but there is no evidence to suggest it does."
His conclusion, in his words: "I would say it has nothing to do with her
death."

The MHRA told the inquest that Melanotan II is unlicensed and has not been
tested for safety, quality or effectiveness, so its possible side-effects
are unknown. The person running Totally Tanning was given a caution and the
site was closed down. The finding was an absence of evidence, not evidence
of absence — and it made Melanotan II's first UK coroner's-court appearance
a verdict about what nobody knows.
