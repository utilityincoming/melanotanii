On 15 April 2026 the FDA confirmed the removal of twelve peptides, including
Melanotan II, from Category 2 of its interim compounding policy after their
nominators withdrew — effective 22 April 2026. The removal lifted Category
2's "may present significant safety risks" designation, but added the
substance to nothing: no category, not the 503A bulks list. For compounding
purposes, Melanotan II became a bulk substance the FDA does not recognise at
all.

The FDA's withdrawn-nominations table retains the assessment that
accompanied MT-II: risk of immunogenicity due to aggregation and peptide
impurities, and — in the agency's own words — "Published case reports discuss
serious adverse events including melanoma, posterior reversible
encephalopathy syndrome, sympathomimetic toxidrome and priapism."

The Pharmaceutical Compounding Advisory Committee deferred MT-II in July 2026,
and the October 2026 interim enforcement-discretion policy excludes it (both
have their own records). The April removal was a procedural step that left
the science questions exactly where they were — on a calendar.
