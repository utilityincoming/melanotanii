The TGA's Freedom of Information disclosure log lists request 26-3029 —
"Adverse event reports associated with peptides" — released on 6 July 2026.
The melanotan document discloses individual case safety reports (ICSRs) at
case level: 89 reports associated with Melanotan II between 2022 and 2025,
including two melanoma cases.

Each report carries a case identifier (AU-TGA-…), the reporter type, the
reaction terms, and the TGA's causality assessment — typically "possible".
The medicine lines name what people actually bought: "MELANOTAN 2 (Unapproved
peptide product)", alongside brand names such as Phatt Tan.

The file carries the regulator's own standard caveat: information from
adverse-event reports is incomplete, and it cannot be used to assess a
medicine's safety. That caveat is the point. These 89 reports are what
Australia's national system collects when a substance circulates with no
market authorisation, no manufacturer pharmacovigilance, and no clinical
trial — a spontaneous-report trickle, causality unresolvable, released only
because someone filed an FOI request.
